KARGO — PM / SENIOR PM SHORTLISTING RUBRIC
=============================================
Purpose: Score all 60 applicants (both roles) on 5 metrics. Metrics are
mutually exclusive (each piece of resume evidence maps to exactly one
metric) and collectively exhaustive (together they cover everything
that distinguished Kargo's "Exceeds Expectations" hires from "Meets"
and "Below", plus everything the two JDs explicitly ask for).

Source: Built from the 8 past-hire profiles (5 Exceeds, 2 Meets, 1
Below) and the PM / Senior PM job descriptions.

Scoring: Each metric scored 1-4. Same 5 metrics apply to both roles;
what counts as a "4" differs by seniority level where noted. Total
score = sum of all 5 (max 20). No metric is optional — a candidate
who scores 0 evidence on a metric gets a 1, not a blank.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
METRIC 1: GROUND-LEVEL DOMAIN GROUNDING (logistics/freight/ops reality)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Definition: Direct, hands-on exposure to how freight forwarding, ports,
customs, or physical operations actually run — not just having sold
or built software adjacent to them.

Why it matters: Present in all 5 "Exceeds" hires (ex-CHA ops exec,
freight documentation, port services sales, freight forwarding client
relations, supply chain/carrier ops) and absent in all 3 non-Exceeds
hires. The JD only names this as a plus for the Senior PM role — the
pattern shows it predicts performance for PM too.

Scoring:
  1 — No logistics/ops/supply-chain exposure anywhere in career.
  2 — Sold or built software for logistics clients, but no hands-on
      operational role.
  3 — Held an operational role in an adjacent domain (e.g. general
      supply chain, e-commerce fulfilment) but not freight/customs/port.
  4 — Held a hands-on operational role directly in freight forwarding,
      customs, port operations, or carrier logistics.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
METRIC 2: SELF-INITIATED OWNERSHIP (built/fixed the unasked-for thing)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Definition: A specific, dated instance of the candidate identifying a
problem nobody assigned them and building or fixing it anyway, under
real constraint (time pressure, no headcount, no mandate) — and it
got adopted by others afterward.

Why it matters: Every Exceeds hire has one concrete instance of this
(a tool built overnight, a workflow redesigned over a weekend, a bug
report nobody asked them to write). It is initiative that precedes
and exceeds the job description, not initiative described in general
terms ("proactive," "self-starter").

Scoring:
  1 — No specific instance found; only generic self-description
      ("proactive," "self-starter") with no concrete example.
  2 — One instance of solving an assigned problem well, but it was
      requested/expected, not self-initiated.
  3 — One self-initiated fix/build, but adoption/impact unclear or
      limited to the candidate's own work.
  4 — One or more dated, specific instances of building/fixing
      something unprompted, under real constraint, that was adopted
      by a team or became standard practice.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
METRIC 3: DECISION AUTONOMY TRACK RECORD (no senior layer catching errors)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Definition: Evidence the candidate's decisions were final in practice —
they were the last checkpoint, not one voice feeding into someone
senior who made the actual call.

Why it matters: Distinct from Metric 2 (which is about initiative/
action). This is about accountability structure — every Exceeds hire
explicitly operated without a layer above absorbing their mistakes
("no escalation to management," "sole PM," "most senior engineer in
the room," "no account manager layer"). Both JDs state this directly
for Kargo itself ("no committee that approves product decisions").

Scoring:
  1 — Always operated inside a structured team with a senior owner
      making final calls (e.g. large PM team with templates, layered
      approvals).
  2 — Contributed to decisions but shared final accountability with a
      manager or peer group.
  3 — Owned a defined area independently, with informal/occasional
      escalation to a manager.
  4 — Explicitly the final decision-maker in their area for an
      extended period, with stated evidence of no escalation layer
      (sole owner, no account manager, no committee).

---------------------------------------------------------------------
METRIC 4: ROLE-CALIBRATED PRODUCT CRAFT (JD-specific competency)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Definition: The explicit, named skill each JD asks for — this is the
one metric where the bar differs by role.

  For PM applicants, look for: discovery work with real users, shipped
  features with adoption evidence, and — critically — features KILLED
  based on data, not just shipped. Comfort working without a PM
  handbook, sprint template, or design system already in place.

  For Senior PM applicants, look for: ownership of integration/
  architecture-level decisions (what to build vs. configure vs. avoid),
  reliability/data-quality accountability, and evidence of shaping how
  a product function works (frameworks, standards, practices for
  others) — not just running one.

Scoring (apply the PM bar to PM applicants, SPM bar to SPM applicants):
  1 — No evidence of discovery, shipping, or prioritization judgment;
      purely execution/delivery of specs handed to them.
  2 — Shipped features with defined process support (existing templates,
      structured discovery, PM team backing) — competent but not
      built for ambiguity.
  3 — Shipped AND killed at least one feature/initiative based on data
      or user evidence, in a reasonably structured environment.
  4 — Shipped, killed, and can point to a specific higher-stakes call
      made in an unstructured environment (PM: 0-to-1 process
      creation; SPM: integration/architecture trade-off with
      multi-year consequences) that others now rely on.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
METRIC 5: SCALE-APPROPRIATE EXPERIENCE & COMPLEXITY HANDLED
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Definition: Whether the candidate's years of experience and the
complexity of what they've managed actually match the seniority level
of the role — separate from what they did (Metric 4) or how
autonomously they did it (Metric 3).

  PM target: 2-4 years of PM experience; ideally at a company building
  a product/function for the first time.
  Senior PM target: 5-8 years of PM experience; ideally including
  platform products, integration layers, or complex technical
  environments, and some early-stage-company exposure.

Scoring:
  1 — Experience level clearly mismatched to the role applied for
      (over- or under-qualified relative to the band), or complexity
      of systems/orgs managed is materially below what the role needs.
  2 — Close to the experience band but complexity handled is
      lower than the role demands (e.g. SPM applicant with no
      integration/platform exposure).
  3 — Squarely within the experience band with complexity roughly
      matched to the role.
  4 — Within (or slightly beyond, without being a flight risk for
      being over-levelled) the experience band, with complexity
      handled that meets or exceeds what the role demands.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW TO USE
---------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Score every applicant 1-4 on all 5 metrics using only what's in
   their CV — don't infer beyond stated evidence.
2. Total score out of 20. Use total to rank within each role (PM pool
   ranked separately from SPM pool — do not compare across roles).
3. Flag any applicant scoring 4 on Metrics 1-3 (domain + initiative +
   autonomy) but low on Metric 5 (experience/scale) — these are
   possible "hidden fit" candidates the job spec alone would filter
   out, similar to how Kargo's own past hires were selected.
4. Flag any applicant scoring high on Metric 4-5 (on-paper role fit)
   but low on Metrics 1-3 — these look like Vikram/Rahul Bose/Preetham:
   spec-matched but may land as "Meets" rather than "Exceeds."
